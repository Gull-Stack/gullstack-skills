# SKILL: Reviews & reputation

How GullStack grows and defends client reputations. Written 2026-08; policy facts
verified then. **[load-bearing]** = legal/platform exposure if wrong. Doctrine:
reviews are the **compounding lever** — they drive Local Pack + E-E-A-T
(seo-master), LSA rank (google-ads §3), and conversion on every surface at once.
One system, run forever, never gamed.

## 1. The rules **[load-bearing — enforcement is live]**

**FTC Consumer Review Rule** (effective Oct 2024; first warning letters Dec 22,
2025; civil penalties up to **$53,088 per violation**):
- No fake or AI-fabricated reviews, ever, in any direction.
- No incentives for reviews — not "for positive reviews only," not gift cards,
  not discounts, not raffle entries. The ask must be free of consideration.
- Insiders (owners, staff, family) don't review, or disclose the affiliation.
- Never suppress, bury, or selectively delete negative reviews; never present a
  company-run site as an independent review platform.

**Google (GBP) policy**, including the April 2026 update:
- **No review gating** — you may not branch happy customers to Google and unhappy
  ones to a private form. Everyone gets the same ask.
- **No quotas** and no directing staff to solicit reviews naming a specific
  employee (April 2026). No on-site review kiosks/pressure ("no pressuring
  customers to review on-site" — a QR they scan later is fine, standing over
  them while they type is not).
- Asking is explicitly allowed. Ask everyone, uniformly, with a direct link.

FiveStars-era habits (gated funnels, incentivized asks) are now enforcement
targets — audit any legacy review tooling a client brings before it keeps running.

## 2. The generation loop

- **Timing beats volume:** ask at the moment of delight — payout handed over
  (resale sell-side), job completed and approved (services), closing day (title).
  Not three weeks later.
- **Mechanics:** direct short-link/QR to the GBP review composer (no "search for
  us and find the button" friction). Printed card at POS + the post-visit
  email/SMS flow (`email-sms-lifecycle` §4.2 owns delivery; this skill owns the
  rules of the ask).
- **Verbal ask script for staff** (allowed; quotas and name-dropping-me asks are
  not): "If we did right by you today, a Google review is the biggest thank-you
  there is." Train it, don't quota it.
- A feedback survey may run **alongside** the review ask — never as a filter in
  front of it. Survey feedback routes to service recovery (§4).

## 3. Response doctrine

- **Respond to 100% of reviews**, positive and negative, within 24–48h. Response
  volume and recency are themselves ranking/conversion signals.
- Owner voice, specific, short. Thank positives without keyword-stuffing.
- **Negative reviews get the Voss treatment** (three-kings framework): label the
  emotion ("Sounds like we frustrated you at pickup — that's on us"), no
  defensiveness, no arguing facts publicly, one concrete make-it-right step, take
  it offline with a named human. The response is written for the *next hundred
  readers*, not for the reviewer.
- Never disclose customer details in a response (medical, financial, transaction
  specifics) — privacy beats winning the exchange.

## 4. Recovery + removal

- **Service recovery before reputation defense:** the survey/complaint channel
  exists so problems surface to the business first — fix fast, and the review
  that follows is often the save. But the review ask still went to everyone (§1).
- **Flag for removal only genuine policy violations:** spam, off-topic rants,
  competitor/ex-employee conflicts, hate speech. Flag via GBP, document, move on.
  Never buy "review removal services," never litigate by DM.
- A bad review with a great response converts better than a suspicious 5.0.
  Target profile *[approx.]*: 4.5–4.9 with steady velocity beats a static 5.0.

## 5. Measurement

Monthly report lines (analytics-reporting §6): review velocity (new reviews/mo),
rating trend, response rate + median response time, and — where wired — GBP
actions (calls/directions) trend alongside. Velocity stalling for 30+ days means
the ask loop broke (staff stopped, flow broke, link died) — check the loop, not
the customers.

## 6. Mistakes (enforce as review checklist)

Gating/branching the ask · any incentive attached · staff quotas or "mention my
name" asks (banned April 2026) · insider reviews · buying reviews or removal ·
ignoring negatives (or answering them angry, or answering with private details) ·
asking only obviously-happy customers (FTC calls this out explicitly) · one big
ask-blast instead of a standing loop · legacy gated tooling left running ·
review velocity unmonitored.

## Worked examples

**Plato's Closet Draper:** the ask rides the sell-side payout moment ("cash in
hand" = peak delight) — card in the payout envelope + post-visit SMS, same ask to
every seller. Buy-side ask rides the fitting-room win via post-visit email.
**OnRecord Title:** ask at closing/funding day to *clients* (realtor/lender/
builder/investor vernacular — the repeat customer, who reviews once but refers
forever) and the transaction parties; responses never reference transaction
details (title privacy beats a good reply).
